Who this applies to

This angle suits borrowers who want an informed broker discussion rather than a single-bank comparison. It fits people with a defined goal who still need help testing lender policy, loan features and timing before an application is lodged.

  • First home buyers: ask how deposit size, eligibility questions and conditional approval affect the buying plan.
  • Refinancers: ask whether rate, consolidation, renovation funding or investment plans drive the change.
  • Investors: ask how fixed versus variable, offset accounts, interest-only repayments and existing equity would be modelled.
  • Pre-approval seekers: ask what documents are needed, how long approval stays useful and what changes before formal approval.

Deposit schemes, guarantees and policy checks

For eligible first home buyers, the practical issue is whether current scheme settings fit the intended property. The Bendigo material points to the Victorian First Home Owner Grant for eligible new home purchases and the federal First Home Guarantee administered by Housing Australia. It also states eligible buyers may purchase with as little as a 5 percent deposit without LMI, subject to price caps reviewed annually. The safer borrower question is not whether a scheme exists in general, but whether the broker has checked current eligibility, current caps and whether the property fits.

  • Confirm whether the property is established or new before assuming grant treatment.
  • Treat a 5 percent deposit path as conditional, not automatic.
  • Ask what happens if valuation, income evidence or debts change before formal approval.

Refinancing and investment trade-offs

A refinancing comparison should include the current lender's offer, discharge or switching considerations, repayment type and features such as an offset account where appropriate. For investment lending, the comparison should test principal and interest against interest-only repayments, fixed against variable rates, and how existing equity is treated. The point of comparing is not to collect product names; it is to see which loan structure survives a change in rates, rental assumptions or personal circumstances.

  • Refinancing: separate a genuine rate saving from short-term cashback noise.
  • Investment: model interest-only, offset use and equity release before choosing a path.
  • Both: ask for the comparison in writing and note the assumptions used.

Timing, documents and approval stages

Two timing anchors matter: pre-approval is typically valid for around 90 days depending on the lender, and application to settlement typically takes around four to six weeks depending on lender and borrower circumstances. Treat these as planning ranges rather than guarantees. Conditional approval is useful before house hunting because it indicates what a lender is likely to lend, but it is not the same as formal approval after a property is found. Prepare identity details, income evidence, existing loan information, savings history, credit commitments and intended use before lenders are compared. A cleaner file helps show where the application is strong and where a lender may ask further questions.

Common questions

Is a no-cost introduction the same as no broker payment? No. The Bendigo guide states the introduction is free and obligation-free, while Australian residential brokers are typically paid lender commission, with exceptions disclosed upfront under ASIC rules. Borrowers should still ask how the broker is paid and whether any fee applies.

How long does pre-approval usually last? The figure given is around 90 days depending on the lender. It is a planning range, not a guarantee, because formal approval still depends on the property, valuation, income evidence, debts and lender assessment.

Can eligible buyers use a 5 percent deposit without LMI? The Bendigo material says eligible first home buyers may buy with as little as a 5 percent deposit without LMI under the federal guarantee, subject to annual price caps.
